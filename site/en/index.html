3 When is a swarm the right tool?

Why look: if a problem does not need a swarm, the team cannot answer "why many robots?". Source: research/lu_qi.md section 2.1.

Şahin (2005) proposed criteria that separate swarm robotics from other multi-robot work. He also listed application domains [S-LQ-23]. This is partial, from search snippets only. The criteria below are the owner's list, not quoted from the paper. Brambilla et al. (2013) note that swarm robotics is still limited as an engineering discipline [S-LQ-24].

Table 7. Conditions where a swarm fits (the reasons are inferences)
CriterionWhy a swarm helps
Large or spread-out areaMany cheap robots cover more ground in parallel
Unknown or changing environmentLocal rules react without a global map
No central infrastructureNo server, no global positioning and no central radio are needed
Robust to failuresLosing a few robots does not stop the task
ScalableAdding robots needs no redesign
Table 8. Does each idea truly need a swarm? (inference)
IdeaNeeds a swarm?Reason
Pheromone foragingyesStigmergy (indirect talk through marks left in the environment) and robustness to bad robots are swarm questions
Precision irrigationyesLarge field, changing need and robot failures. A central planner is the baseline
Spiral searchpartlyScalability is the question. One robot is the lower bound
Evolved controllerpartlyLearning is the focus. The swarm is the test bed
Risk-aware navigationyesRobot loss is expected. Sharing danger information is the swarm benefit
Territory gamepartlyIt is a team game. A central team planner could also play
Sorting and segregationyesThere is no leader. Only local rules apply
Collective decisionyesCollective sensing beats one noisy robot
HerdingpartlyOne shepherd can herd a flock [S-LQ-10]. Many shepherds help with large flocks [S-LQ-12]
