1 Problem

  • A fixed spiral search (a path that winds outward from one point to sweep a region) finds nearby food first. But it scales poorly when the swarm grows.
  • A random walk (movement with random direction changes) scales well with a big swarm, but it wastes time.
  • partial The two sentences above come from a search snippet of the paper abstract [S-LQ-5].

2 Goal

  • Rebuild a search that covers many regions with bidirectional spirals.
  • Compare it with the random walk search as the swarm size grows.

3 How it works in ARGoS

Robots4 to 32 foot-bots (the stock wheeled robot model of ARGoS). One nest sits in the center.

Behavior rulesEach region has spiral waypoints to follow. The two robots of one region start from opposite ends of the spiral. partial From a search snippet [S-LQ-5]
Loop functionsLoop functions (user code that runs at every simulation step) place the food. There are three layouts: uniform, clustered and power-law. They also count floor points and delivered food [S-LQ-9].

4 Algorithm steps

  1. Step 1 Split regions
  2. Step 2 Assign regions
  3. Step 3 Bidirectional spiral
  4. Step 4 Deliver food
  5. Step 5 Baseline
Step 1 of 5

Divide the arena into k square regions of equal size.

Step 2 of 5

Assign each robot to an empty region or to a region with only one robot. partial [S-LQ-5]

Step 3 of 5

Robot 1 walks the spiral of its region outward. Robot 2 walks it inward.

Step 4 of 5

On finding food, the robot goes to the nest and drops it. Then it returns to the last spiral point.

Step 5 of 5 (baseline)

The baseline is a correlated random walk (a random walk with persistent heading) that returns to the nest.

5 What to measure

  • Measure food collected in a fixed time, against swarm size (4, 8, 16, 32) and food layout.
  • Find the crossover swarm size where the random walk starts to beat the spiral.

6 Research potential and venues

  • Open question: when does a fixed plan beat a stochastic plan, as swarm size and food distribution change [S-LQ-5].
  • Beyond a demo: a hybrid rule that switches from spiral to random walk when a crowding signal arrives (inference).
  • Venue candidates (inference): Conference on Robots and Vision (CRV, a robotics and vision conference), International Conference on Swarm Intelligence (ANTS, a swarm intelligence conference), Distributed Autonomous Robotic Systems (DARS, a distributed robotics conference).
  • Google Scholar lists CRV as the venue of Prof. Lu's spiral paper [S-LQ-3].
