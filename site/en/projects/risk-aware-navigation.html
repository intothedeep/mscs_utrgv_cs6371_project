1 Problem

  • A swarm must cross a field to reach a goal region.
  • Parts of the field disable robots.
  • Nobody knows where the danger is at the start. The swarm learns by losing robots or by sensing.

2 Goal

  • Share danger as robots find it, so the largest fraction of robots reaches the goal alive.

3 How it works in ARGoS

Table 1. ARGoS setup (source: source text, How it works in ARGoS)
ItemContent
Robots20 to 40 foot-bots (the wheeled robot model that ARGoS provides). A start region and a goal region are painted on the floor.
SensorsPositioning sensor, proximity sensor, and range-and-bearing sensor (reads neighbor distance and direction and passes short messages) for danger messages. The ground sensor is optional and reads hazard hints.
Variant (a) hazard zonesThe zones are hidden. The loop function disables a robot inside a zone with probability p per step.
Variant (b) surveillanceSentries with a view cone stand still or patrol. A robot inside a cone is detected. It is lost, or the mission fails.
Loop functionsLoop functions (user code that runs at every ARGoS simulation step) handle the hazard logic, the sentry logic and the kill list. They draw a shared risk grid on the floor as a "danger pheromone" (a virtual mark that fades with time) [S-LQ-9].

4 Algorithm steps

  1. Step 1 A* planning
  2. Step 2 Where robots fell
  3. Step 3 Share danger
  4. Step 4 Replan
  5. Step 5 Sweep w
  6. Baselines
Step 1 of 5

Each robot plans a path on a grid with A* path search (an algorithm that finds the cheapest path on a grid). Edge cost is distance plus w times the risk of the cell.

Step 2 of 5

When a robot is disabled, its last cell gets high risk. Neighbors heard its last messages.

Step 3 of 5

Surviving robots broadcast risk updates over range-and-bearing. Or the loop function paints them as a danger pheromone that decays.

Step 4 of 5

A robot replans when its path crosses a cell whose risk rose.

Step 5 of 5

Sweep w from 0 (shortest path) to a high value (most cautious).

Baselines

  • Individual shortest path (w = 0, no sharing).
  • Plain flocking toward the goal (neighbors stay close and move the same way).
  • Swarm with danger sharing.

5 What to measure

  • Measure the survival fraction and the time to goal.
  • Draw the trade-off curve. Each w value gives one point. The x axis is time and the y axis is survival.
