1 Problem

  • A hand-tuned foraging rule breaks when obstacles appear.
  • Prof. Lu's Scholar list has a paper that evolves a foraging swarm in an obstacle environment with NeuroEvolution of Augmenting Topologies (NEAT, a method that grows the weights and structure of a neural network by evolution) [S-LQ-3].
  • partial The author list was not read.

2 Goal

  • Evolve a small neural network or parametric controller for foraging with obstacles.
  • Compare it with a hand-written controller.

4 Algorithm steps

  1. Step 1 Take the frame
  2. Step 2 Genome
  3. Step 3 Fitness
  4. Step 4 Evolve
  5. Step 5 Test
Step 1 of 5

Start from the structure of the mpga example [S-LQ-7].

Step 2 of 5

The genome (the set of values that evolution changes) holds the weights of a small fixed neural network. It is simpler than NEAT.

Step 3 of 5

Fitness (the score of one genome) is the food delivered in one trial. Average it over 3 seeds.

Step 4 of 5

Evolve for G generations. The owner runs it.

Step 5 of 5

Test the best genome on an unseen obstacle map.

5 What to measure

  • Measure the best fitness and the mean fitness in every generation.
  • On unseen maps, compare the food collected by the evolved controller and the hand-written controller.

6 Research potential and venues

  • Open question: does an evolved swarm controller transfer to new obstacle layouts (inference).
  • Beyond a demo: a transfer test across map types (inference).
  • Venue candidates (inference): International Conference on Swarm Intelligence (ANTS, a swarm intelligence conference), a workshop of the Genetic and Evolutionary Computation Conference (GECCO, an evolutionary computation conference), Artificial Life (ALIFE, an artificial life conference).
