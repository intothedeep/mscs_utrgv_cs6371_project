1 Problem

  • A swarm must pick the better of n options with noisy local sensing.
  • Example: pick the color that covers more of the floor.

2 Goal

  • Foot-bots estimate the dominant floor color and reach consensus.
  • Measure the accuracy and the time it takes.

3 How it works in ARGoS

Table 1. ARGoS setup (source: source text, How it works in ARGoS)
ItemContent
Robots20 to 50 foot-bots (the wheeled robot model that ARGoS provides).
SensorsThe ground sensor reads black and white cells. The range-and-bearing sensor (reads neighbor distance and direction and passes short messages) shares opinions.
Behavior rulesExplore, sample, then vote. The rule is the majority rule (follow the opinion held by more neighbors) or the voter model (copy one randomly chosen neighbor).
Loop functionsLoop functions (user code that runs at every ARGoS simulation step) build a random black and white tile floor with a set ratio [S-LQ-9].

4 Algorithm steps

  1. Step 1 Sample
  2. Step 2 Opinion
  3. Step 3 Broadcast
  4. Step 4 Update
  5. Step 5 Stop
Step 1 of 5

Each robot samples the floor color for a time window.

Step 2 of 5

It forms an opinion from the samples.

Step 3 of 5

It broadcasts its opinion and hears its neighbors.

Step 4 of 5

It updates its opinion by the majority of its neighbors.

Step 5 of 5

Stop when 90% of the robots agree.

5 What to measure

  • Measure accuracy and time to consensus against the floor ratio (0.52 to 0.7) and the sensor noise.

6 Research potential and venues

  • Open question: decisions with imperfect sensors [S-LQ-16], and with only a subset of the swarm [S-LQ-17].
  • New beyond a demo: a noise-versus-accuracy curve (inference).
  • Venues (inference): International Conference on Swarm Intelligence (ANTS), International Symposium on Swarm Behavior and Bio-Inspired Robotics (SWARM), and workshops at the International Conference on Intelligent Robots and Systems (IROS).
