1 Problem

  • Robots must drive passive or reactive agents to a goal.
  • One shepherd uses two modes. "Collect" gathers stragglers. "Drive" pushes the group [S-LQ-10].

2 Goal

  • Foot-bot shepherds push a flock of foot-bot "sheep" into a goal pen.
  • Compare 1, 2 and 4 shepherds.

3 How it works in ARGoS

Table 1. ARGoS setup (source: source text, How it works in ARGoS)
ItemContent
Robots20 sheep foot-bots (the wheeled robot model that ARGoS provides), and 1 to 4 shepherd foot-bots.
SensorsThe range-and-bearing sensor (reads neighbor distance and direction) lets sheep sense shepherds and each other. The positioning sensor is also used.
Behavior rulesSheep move away from shepherds and toward the flock centroid. The shepherd uses Strömbom's collect and drive [S-LQ-10].
Loop functionsLoop functions (user code that runs at every ARGoS simulation step) make the goal pen on the floor and count the sheep inside it.

4 Algorithm steps

  1. Step 1 Sheep
  2. Step 2 Collect
  3. Step 3 Drive
  4. Step 4 Many shepherds
  5. Step 5 Stop
Step 1 of 5

A sheep moves away from shepherds within radius r and toward the local centroid, and noise is added.

Step 2 of 5

If the farthest sheep is too far from the centroid, the shepherd goes behind that sheep (collect).

Step 3 of 5

Otherwise the shepherd goes behind the centroid, on the side opposite the pen (drive) [S-LQ-10].

Step 4 of 5 (variant)

Split the sheep by angle and give each sector one shepherd.

Step 5 of 5

Stop when 90% of the sheep are inside the pen.

5 What to measure

  • Measure the time to put 90% of the sheep in the pen against the number of shepherds and sheep.

6 Research potential and venues
