1 Problem

  • A swarm must split mixed items or robots into groups without a leader. Each group gathers in its own place.
  • Ant brood sorting inspired a simple rule: pick up lonely items and drop them next to similar ones (Deneubourg et al., 1991) [UNVERIFIED].

2 Goal

  • Sort two item colors into two clusters, or separate two robot types.
  • Measure cluster purity (the share of the main color inside a cluster, where 100% means one color only) over time.

3 How it works in ARGoS

Table 1. ARGoS setup (source: source text, How it works in ARGoS)
ItemContent
Robots10 to 20 foot-bots (the wheeled robot model that ARGoS provides).
SensorsThe ground sensor reads the item color, which is placed as a floor spot. The positioning sensor is also used.
Behavior rulesThe pick-up probability falls when many similar items are nearby. The drop probability rises when many are nearby.
Loop functionsLoop functions (user code that runs at every ARGoS simulation step) place items as colored floor spots. They count the local density around each item [S-LQ-9].
Robot segregation variantGive different attraction to the same type and to the other type (differential potentials, which attract or repel by type so types separate) [S-LQ-15].

4 Algorithm steps

  1. Step 1 Scatter
  2. Step 2 Fraction f
  3. Step 3 Pick up
  4. Step 4 Drop
  5. Step 5 Variant
Step 1 of 5

Scatter items of two colors. The source text uses red and blue.

Step 2 of 5

A robot walks at random and stops on an item. It computes the fraction f of nearby items with the same color. The loop function supplies this value.

Step 3 of 5

It picks up the item with the probability below. The form of the formula is attributed to Deneubourg et al. 1991, and the source was not read [UNVERIFIED].

p_pick = (k1 / (k1 + f))^2

Step 4 of 5

It carries the item. It drops it where the same-color fraction is high, with the probability below [UNVERIFIED].

p_drop = (f / (k2 + f))^2

Step 5 of 5 (variant)

Put a nest for each color. The structure is like Prof. Lu's multiple-place foraging [S-LQ-2].

5 What to measure

  • Measure the number of clusters and the purity for each color over time.
  • Measure the time to reach 90% purity against swarm size.

6 Research potential and venues

  • Open question: how fast and how pure is sorting with minimal sensing (inference).
  • New beyond a demo: sorting into fixed depots that the swarm chooses (inference).
  • Venues (inference): International Conference on Swarm Intelligence (ANTS) and International Symposium on Swarm Behavior and Bio-Inspired Robotics (SWARM).
